Six o'clock lays its orange down
on the windowsill like a coin.

The roses lean into the last of it,
broke gamblers at a warm table.

Somewhere a screen door, somewhere
a sprinkler saying *shh, shh, shh*

to a lawn that never listens.
The sky goes cloud, azul, mimosa —

a bar menu of a sunset,
and we drink it on the steps,

licking the salt of the day
from the rim of the evening.

Stay out, stay out. The porch light
can wait. The moths can wait.

Even the dark, which is patient
and owed everything, can wait.
