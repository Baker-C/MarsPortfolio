The commission was unusual: a map of a garden eleven meters square, at a scale generous enough to name things.

"Whose garden?" Ines asked.

"Mine," said the old man, and paid in advance.

She began, as she always began, with edges. The brick path, the water butt, the bean rows with their string rigging like a small harbor. A morning's work, in truth. She could have drafted it by lunch and been done. But the old man had asked for a *cartographer*, not a draftsman, and there is an oath implied in the title.

So she stayed. She mapped the shade first, because shade is the honest geography of a garden — the cold country under the north wall where only ferns held citizenship, the noon desert by the greenhouse where the thyme ran its little empire. She marked the routes: the snail road along the drainpipe, used at dawn and dusk; the robin's three fixed perches, from which all digging was supervised; the desire line the fox had pressed through the currants, older, she suspected, than the fence it ignored.

The map grew. She added a second sheet, then a third, at larger scale, for the pond. The pond alone was an archipelago — lily pads named like islands, the dragonfly's patrol drawn in dashed blue, the one deep spot where something unseen made rings at evening, marked, in the end, *Here be somebody*.

Weeks in, the old man brought tea out and asked, mildly, if she was nearly finished.

"Nearly started," said Ines, and it was the truth. She had that morning discovered that the garden was different by moonlight — that the night-scented stock threw open borders the day never knew, that moths ran a ferry service the bees would have envied.

The final map was nine sheets and folded to nothing useful. It hung, instead, across one wall of the old man's kitchen, and he read it the way other people read winter catalogs: slowly, in the evenings, mouthing the names.

"You could have charged me for a week," he said once. "It took you a season."

"It took the season it took," said Ines. "Small places are not small. They are just close together."
